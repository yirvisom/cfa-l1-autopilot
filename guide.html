01 / HOW IT WORKS

Three decisions that make this different

Most CFA plans fail for one of three reasons: they plan at topic level, they count reading as studying, or they never measure what was actually learned. This system fixes all three.

01 · GRANULARITY

Every module has a budget

The 102 learning modules each carry learn / practice / review hours. A day is never "study fixed income" — it is LM56, 50 minutes, then 20 questions.

02 · SEQUENCE

Learn once, then practise

Nov 2026 – Jul 2027 is the learn pass. Jul – Nov 2027 is the official practice pass. Then timed sets and mocks. No third full read.

03 · MEASUREMENT

The log is the source of truth

Minutes per module, a score per question set, a why-line per miss, a score per mock. Unlogged study did not happen.

Learnread + notes
Practisequestions, timed
Correctevery miss + why
Review1 / 3 / 7 / 21 days
Mockfull conditions

The honest number: ~9 CFA hours per week. The 30 h/week ceiling in profile.json is a ceiling, not a target. The lesson budget needs about 9 h/week on average, with peaks of 19 h in the July 2027 holiday. The spare capacity is insurance against M2 exams, the PFE and missed weeks.

03 / THE DAILY LOOP

One hour of decisions, not three hours of guilt

The system tells you what to do before you have to think about it. Your only job is to execute the named module and log it honestly.

  • 06:30
    English, 60 min. 30 min reading + 30 min speaking aloud. It is inside the weekly budget and is never cancelled.
    This is exam-reading training disguised as language practice.
  • 07:30
    Run cfa today. Read the target, the module ids, the due reviews and the next deadline. Close the terminal.
    Do not plan the day by hand. The plan is already made.
  • 19:30
    One 60–75 min CFA block. The task names the module and the action. Phone in another room, one tab open.
    Term: five weekday evenings, plus weekend mornings. Holiday: add 09:00–12:00.
  • after
    Log it immediately. cfa log "LM56 20 duration questions" 50 --lesson LM56 --score 70
    One row per session. Score only when it was a question set.
  • 22:30
    Close the day. cfa nudge --final or cfa status. If you did not finish, move it explicitly — never silently.
    A missed task that is moved is data. A missed task that is forgotten is a hole.

Never log reading as practice. If you read for 50 minutes and answered no questions, log 50 minutes against the module with no score. The analytics will show the difference between coverage and accuracy — and that difference is the whole game.

What a correct log looks like

# a learn session: minutes against the module, no score
cfa log "LM59 term structure, read + own notes" 55 --lesson LM59

# a practice set: score it, so accuracy becomes measurable
cfa log "LM56 20 bond pricing questions" 45 --lesson LM56 --score 65

# a spaced review: still the same module id
cfa log "LM93 review: 10 misses + flashcards" 20 --lesson LM93

# a mixed timed set in the practice phase
cfa log "FI timed set 30q" 60 --topic "fixed income" --score 72
04 / THE WEEKLY LOOP

Sunday, 20 minutes, then close the laptop

The week is generated on Sunday evening from the curriculum, your log, the due reviews and your open errors. You do not plan from scratch.

SUNDAY · 19:00

The ritual

1. cfa review — planned vs logged, last week.
2. cfa due — what the spacing schedule wants.
3. cfa errors — what is still open.
4. cfa week --next — build next week.
5. Read the plan once. Do not edit it.

WHAT THE PLAN CONTAINS

Modules, not topics

Each week names the LM ids it covers, the minutes per day, the English block, a checkpoint and the mock calendar. If the model is unavailable, the deterministic fallback still names the right modules.

The 60% rule. If a week lands below 60% of planned minutes, next week cuts the lowest-value task. It never adds hours to catch up. The budget does not move; the content does.

05 / THE STUDY METHOD

How to make a module stick

The hour budget only works if the hour is spent correctly. Use this five-step loop for every module. It is slower on the first page and much faster by the mock.

STEP 1 · 5 MIN

Preview

Read the learning outcomes and the module summary. Write down three questions you expect the module to answer. This gives the reading a target.

STEP 2 · 40–70 MIN

Learn once

One pass. Own-words notes, formula-sheet entry, no highlighting, no copying the curriculum. If you cannot say it in your own words, you have not learned it.

STEP 3 · 20–40 MIN

Practise immediately

15–25 end-of-module questions, closed book, timed. Do not read the answer key while solving. Mark guesses; they are misses.

STEP 4 · 15–25 MIN

Correct everything

For every miss, write one why-line: what rule did I use, why was it wrong, what is the trigger next time. Redo the question from scratch. This is the highest-value 20 minutes in the system.

STEP 5 · 15–25 MIN, ON A SCHEDULE

Space it

Review the module 1, 3, 7 and 21 days later. A review is not a re-read: 10 missed questions, 10 flashcards, then explain the mechanism aloud as if teaching it. cfa due tells you which modules are due; log the review against the same LM id, which advances the interval.

Why this works

Retrieval practice (trying to recall before looking) builds stronger memory than re-reading. Spacing forces recall when it is slightly hard, which is where learning happens. Immediate correction stops a wrong method from becoming automatic. Interleaving — mixing topics in timed sets — trains the exam skill of choosing the right tool, not just using it.

Anti-patterns that feel productive

Exam technique to train from day one

90 seconds per question. Two-pass strategy: answer everything you can in the first pass, flag the rest, return with 20 minutes left. Never leave a blank. Train the timing in every Saturday set — a correct answer that takes four minutes is a loss on a 180-question paper.

06 / THE ERROR LOG

Your error log is the real syllabus

The curriculum tells you what can be asked. The error log tells you what you get wrong. By the practice phase, the error log should drive more of your week than the reading list.

HOW TO WRITE A WHY-LINE

Three parts, one sentence each

What I did: "I used the coupon date instead of the settlement date."
Why it is wrong: "Accrued interest runs from the last coupon to settlement."
Trigger: "When I see clean price, check settlement first."

CADENCE

Add in correction, clear in review

Add during the correction block of every practice set. Review open items for 5 minutes at the start of each session. Close an item only when you can redo the original question from scratch, without notes.

# add a miss
cfa error "confused accrued interest and clean price" --lesson LM56 --why "used coupon date instead of settlement"

# see what is still open
cfa errors

# close it when you can redo it cold
cfa close 4

Target: zero items older than 7 days by the final phase. An error that stays open for a month is not an error log, it is a diary.

BEFORE

Exam conditions

Same time of day as the real slot, 4.5 hours, no pause, no notes, one screen. Water. No phone. Treat it like the fee is already paid.

AFTER

Four hours of correction

Every question: right for the right reason, right by luck, or wrong. Classify each miss as knowledge, method, reading or time. Log the score and the top three causes.

NEXT WEEK

One change only

Below 55%: one remediation week on the two weakest topics. Above target: keep the plan, add timed sets, do not add modules.

cfa mock 68 --note "FI slow, Ethics clean, ran out of time on Q142-160"
cfa mock            # history, average, trend, next scheduled mock

The readiness index

cfa analytics computes a 0–100 index from three measured components: module coverage (40%), practice accuracy (35%) and mock average (25%). Missing components are reweighted, not treated as zero. It is a coaching index, not a predicted pass probability — use it to see which lever is short, not to feel good or bad.

08 / ADAPTATION

What to do when the week goes wrong

The plan is designed to bend. These are the only allowed moves. Anything else becomes a backlog, and backlogs kill 18-month plans.

SituationMove
A week below 60% of minutesCut the lowest-value task next week. Never add hours to catch up.
Behind the module schedule by > 3 modulesProtect the learn block; drop the optional practice extras.
Ahead of scheduleDo not add new modules. Add question sets and bring the next mock forward one week.
Mock below 55%One week of pure remediation on the two weakest topics, then resume. No date changes.
Same topic below 60% twiceSwitch resource: own book → official curriculum, or the reverse.
Two consecutive missed weeksDrop to 3 h/week for one week to restart the habit. Do not attempt the backlog.
M2 exams (Jan and May 2028)January is a 5 h/week maintenance block. May is taper-only.
Registration not done by 14 Oct 2027Stop the study plan. Fix the deadline first.

The one fatal failure is silence. If you fall off for a week, run cfa status, log what actually happened, and let the next plan adapt. The system can only help with the truth.

12 / TROUBLESHOOTING

When something does not fire

cfa or cockpit is not recognised

The shell was opened before the profile/theme was installed. Run . $PROFILE, or open a new tab. Verify with Get-Command cfa, cockpit.

The weekly plan is generic and says “model unavailable”

The DeepSeek key is missing or invalid in ~/.config/cfa/env. The offline fallback is still valid and names the right modules; paste the key to get the adaptive version.

No notifications arrive

On Fedora the timers use notify-send. On Windows the theme writes to ~/.local/state/cfa/inbox.log; the scheduled tasks can be registered with install-cfa-tasks.ps1. Every message is also in the inbox file.

A quote says NO TAPE

Yahoo did not answer within the timeout, or the symbol is wrong. The rest of the desk is unaffected. Try quote AAPL or use the correct ticker (BTC-USD, EURUSD=X).

The exam date or a deadline changes

Edit profile.json (single source of truth), then run cfa week --force. The countdown, phases and checkpoints follow.

I missed two weeks

Do not attempt the backlog. Run cfa status, log what happened, and follow the two-week rule: 3 h/week for one week to restart the habit, then resume the current week. The plan has slack for this.

I want to study more than the plan

Add question sets, not new modules, and bring the next mock forward one week. The plan is already 730 h; more reading is not the constraint.
